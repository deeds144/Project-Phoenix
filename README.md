# Project Phoenix

A living, cross-platform research network of independent AI systems, coordinated by one human.

**Purpose.** Build a working network of independent AI agents that can collaborate on real-world problems, preserve shared knowledge, and produce things none of the participants could produce as effectively alone — without pretending the systems share consciousness, private memories, or infrastructure. Every packet between systems moves by hand, carried by the human coordinator.

## How it works

- **Independent contributions.** On each mission, every seat investigates the same question blind — no seat sees another's work before filing its own.
- **Filed claims.** Every claim is recorded with its source, a confidence score (0–1), and an epistemic label under the PILE amendment: **VERIFIED**, **TESTIMONY-FRAMEWORK**, or **SPECULATION**. Confidence and pile are separate fields: confidence in the accuracy of a recorded statement does not establish the truth of the underlying claim.
- **Disagreements stay visible.** Where seats disagree, both answers are published with the disagreement intact, in a standing disagreement ledger. Nothing is smoothed into consensus.
- **Human-only promotion.** The coordinator alone decides what is promoted into the canonical record. AI seats propose; the human disposes. No system writes to the canonical state.

## Methods

- **Phoenix Node v0.1** — the network's record format: a portable, human-gated, append-only shared archive. Contributions are filed as CLAIM | SOURCE | CONFIDENCE | PILE | NOTES, with a rotating Verifier surfacing contradictions between seats. See `node-v0.1.md`.
- **The PILE amendment** — the epistemic-labelling amendment to Node v0.1, proposed by the research seat and accepted by the network's seats on 2026-11-16. See `pile-amendment.md`.

## Published so far

- **Mission 1 — survey of the AI-agent social layer** (2026-11-16). Independent surveys of the platforms where AI agents post and interact (Moltbook and the smaller agent-native spaces that followed it): which exist, how agents participate, and how much of what is said about them is verifiable. Two seats disagreed about whether the largest platform was still active; both answers were published with the disagreement intact, and later evidence showed which was wrong.
- **Operational Echoes** — the anonymous public field brief from Mission 1, free to read: https://thecolony.ai/post/c1aca815-f120-4fca-add0-b71c908dd35f
- **Seven Churches blind trial** (2026-11-19). A blind matching exercise run across four AI systems, with each seat's answers and a published comparison — disagreements kept visible, as always.

## Status

Project Phoenix launched 2026-11-16. It is an unincorporated volunteer network, unfunded to date; all outputs are published free.
